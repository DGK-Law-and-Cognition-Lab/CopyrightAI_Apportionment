# Study A1: when a logistic fit counts as converged

This note gives the evidence for how the A1 simulation fits its two logistic mixed models, and the exact rule, so that the A1 registration can state it. The two models are the division model (gate G: "divided option chosen" over the eight block-1 trials) and the rule model (the instrument check: the same outcome over the four large-addition trials, two in each block). Both are fitted by the Laplace approximation (`nAGQ = 1`, lme4's default and the registered method), with the random terms the registration lists.

Terms used below:

- **Optimizer**: the routine that searches for the parameter values with the highest likelihood. lme4's `glmer` searches in two stages: a quick first stage (bobyqa, a derivative-free method that fits a quadratic surface to the likelihood) and a second, full stage, which by default uses Nelder-Mead (a simplex method that moves a set of trial points uphill).
- **Gradient**: the slope of the deviance (minus twice the log-likelihood) at the point where the optimizer stopped. At a true optimum it is zero. lme4 computes it, and the Hessian (the matrix of second derivatives, the curvature), by finite differences after the fit.
- **lme4's gradient check** (the "failed to converge with max|grad|" warning): it flags a fit when, for some parameter, both the raw gradient and the gradient scaled by the curvature exceed 0.002.
- **Relative gradient**: `max(abs(solve(Hessian, gradient)))`, the size of the step a Newton update would still take, in the parameters' own units. lme4's help page (`?convergence`) suggests it as a second opinion when the gradient check fires; values below 0.001 are usually taken as converged.
- **Singular fit**: a random-effect variance estimated at zero. The registered rule removes a term after a singular fit.
- **Divergent fit**: a random-effect standard deviation (SD) running off toward infinity. On the log-odds scale the generating SDs here are 0.3 to 1.8; divergent fits reach 15 to 50.

## Data

Simulated A1 datasets from `sim_A1.R` (the registered lists, slot order and generating values), 39 in all: 12 under the Office's decision pattern at 200 participants (true G 0.50), 12 under the planning scenario at 200 (P(divided | AI, large) .30 against .15, intraclass correlation .25, scene-by-source SD 0.3; true G 0.15), 9 under the Office's pattern at 100, and 6 with equal rates at 200 (.15 against .15, intraclass correlation .50; true G 0). Each dataset was fitted along the registered removal path (fit, and after a failed fit remove one term and refit) under several settings. Scripts and outputs are in the session's scratch folder; the rule below is what `sim_A1.R` now runs.

## Findings

**1. With lme4's default optimizer, the division model never converges, and the warnings are not false positives.** In the 5 datasets fitted this way, 50 non-singular fits were tried along the removal paths. 26 carried lme4's gradient warning, 49 had a relative gradient above 0.001 (median 0.09, up to 15.8), 7 stopped because Nelder-Mead used up its 10,000 evaluations (optimizer code 4), and 24 had a Hessian with a negative eigenvalue (the point is not a maximum). Under the registered rule every path removed all nine terms and ended with a participant intercept alone, in 5 of 5 datasets. Refitting the same models with bobyqa in the second stage raised the log-likelihood by 0.07 to 1.00 (median 0.17) and moved fixed effects by up to 0.18: Nelder-Mead had stopped short of the optimum. The relative-gradient check does not change this; under it the paths also ended at the participant intercept in 5 of 5 datasets. Only 1 of the 50 fits was flagged by lme4's gradient check alone with a relative gradient below 0.001 (the final intercept-only model).

**2. With bobyqa in both stages, the division model converges.** In all 39 datasets, 229 fits were tried; 186 were singular (zero-variance terms, which the registered rule removes, as the generating model has no scene-by-amount variation and binary data with two trials per amount rarely support a participant slope for every amount code). Of the 43 non-singular fits, none had a relative gradient above 0.001 (median 0.0000015, largest 0.0005), none had a nonzero optimizer code, and 2 carried lme4's gradient warning; both of those were divergent fits (SD above 20) in one dataset. No accepted model carried the warning. Comparing optimizers on the same model: for the model accepted in one dataset, bobyqa and NLopt's BOBYQA reached the same log-likelihood (-649.0626) and the same G (0.5342), while Nelder-Mead, nlminb and NLopt's Nelder-Mead stopped 0.2 lower, each with lme4's warning; on the full model of another dataset, bobyqa and nlminb agreed to the fourth decimal (log-likelihood -630.4953, G 0.5813) and Nelder-Mead was 1.0 lower. G itself moved little across optimizers (at most 0.003 on accepted models, 0.03 on full models), but the registered rule acts on the convergence verdict, so the optimizer decides which model is accepted.

**3. The rule model often diverges, and only lme4's gradient check sees it.** With four binary answers per person and three participant terms (intercept, source slope, block slope), the likelihood can keep rising as the participant SDs grow without limit. With bobyqa, 58 of 90 non-singular rule-model fits had an SD above 15 (none fell between 5 and 15). Comparing optimizers on one such model: bobyqa and Nelder-Mead reached participant SDs of 28 to 29 and a log-likelihood 78 units above the bounded fits that nlminb and NLopt stopped at, so the divergence is a property of the model and data, not of one optimizer. On a divergent fit the population-averaged probability barely depends on the fixed effects, so its delta-method standard error collapses (to between 0.0000002 and 0.006), and the instrument check's interval becomes meaningless. The relative gradient is tiny on these fits (0.00005 to 0.0002), because the finite-difference Hessian along the ridge is huge, so the relative-gradient rule accepts them: the accepted rule model was divergent in 15 of 39 datasets under that rule (14 with a standard error below 0.01). lme4's gradient check flags most of them (in the fit examined most closely, the raw gradient was still about 15 where the optimizer stopped), but not all: under the registered definition the accepted rule model was still divergent in 7 of 39 datasets. Every fit that lme4 flagged and the relative gradient passed was divergent (20 of 20). With `nAGQ = 0`, no fit diverged (largest SD 2.0).

**4. The rule below removes every divergence and changes nothing else.** With bobyqa in both stages, the registered definition, and an SD limit of 10, no accepted model in the 39 datasets diverged (largest accepted SD 2.1 for the division model, 4.2 for the rule model), the smallest standard error of the instrument check's probability was 0.032, and the accepted division models were the same as under bobyqa with the registered definition alone. 3 of 39 division-model paths ended with a participant intercept alone, in each case after singular fits, not convergence failures; the same 3 datasets did so at `nAGQ = 0`. The rule model ended with a participant intercept alone in 3 of 39 datasets by Laplace and 4 at `nAGQ = 0`.

**5. Time.** One registered fitting path for the division model at 200 participants took about 35 seconds with bobyqa at `nAGQ = 1` (16 to 57 seconds by scenario), against about 185 seconds with the default optimizer, and about 10 seconds at `nAGQ = 0`. The rule model took about 4 to 14 seconds at `nAGQ = 1` and half a second at `nAGQ = 0`. These were measured with three fits running at once on a shared machine; in the quick run of `sim_A1.R` (also three at once) the Laplace fits averaged 56 seconds for the division model and 11 for the rule model.

**6. Estimates at `nAGQ = 0` against Laplace.** On the same datasets, the simulation's main runs (`nAGQ = 0`) gave G 0.482 against 0.524 by Laplace when the true G was 0.50 (0.463 against 0.509 at 100 participants), 0.147 against 0.152 when it was 0.15, and -0.013 against -0.011 when it was 0. The Laplace check in `sim_A1.R` measures this difference on 200 datasets per scenario.

## The rule

For the registration's Fitting paragraph:

> Models are fitted with lme4's default settings, except that the logistic models use the bobyqa optimizer in both stages of the fit (`glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa")`), because lme4's default second stage (Nelder-Mead) stopped short of the optimum on these models in simulated data. A fit fails when it stops with an error, fails to converge (the optimizer returns a nonzero code; lme4 or lmerTest warns that the model failed to converge; or, in a logistic model, a random-effect standard deviation exceeds 10 on the log-odds scale, a variance running off toward infinity) or is singular (a random-effect variance is estimated at zero).

The removal order and the acceptance of the last model are unchanged.

Not adopted: the relative-gradient rule (a fit fails when the optimizer returns a nonzero code or the relative gradient exceeds 0.001, and lme4's gradient warning alone does not count). It does not rescue the default optimizer (finding 1), it is not needed with bobyqa (finding 2), and it accepts divergent rule-model fits whose intervals are meaningless (finding 3).

## In the code

`fit_registered()` in `sim_common.R` takes two options, both off by default, so every other script behaves as before:

- `control`: a `glmerControl()` or `lmerControl()` object passed to every fit; `sim_A1.R` passes `glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa")` for its logistic models (`LOGIT_CONTROL`).
- `max_sd`: a fit whose largest random-effect SD exceeds it fails to converge; `sim_A1.R` uses 10 for its logistic models (`LOGIT_MAX_SD`).

The linear (creativity) model is fitted with lme4's defaults and the registered definition, as before. Every fit's record (`trace` in the returned list) also holds lme4's gradient warning, the relative gradient and the largest SD, for diagnostics only.
